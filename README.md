# AI Cross-Jurisdictional Review
## Research Question
How does United states of America, New Zeland England, Canada, and Australia's AI policy compare to the OECD and G7 Hiroshima Ai Process [G7 Hiro]? What is lacking? And what other themes are found within these Countries? 
### Process
I prompted AI (Microsoft Co-pilot) to scan OECD and G7 Hiro to pull out themes. Simplifying the themes into single or couple words.
I then looked at various levels of governments (13) within the respective countries on their respective websites that hold information regarding AI policy and practice. Looking mainly regarding the headings and subheading, I documented on Excel through counts the amount of times these themes occurred. Additionally, I added other themes that were novel when scanning the documentation. 
I then quantified the results, creating percentages of the themes in the respective topics ( OECD, G7 Hiro, Other).

## Find Summary
The main themes that stand out are the themes that coincide with the OECD and the G7 is  Accountability, Transparency, Human oversight and Explain-ability. There is a overall expectation of human oversight in keeping governments accountability that focuses on safety when considering how AI will be deployed, developed and maintained. These themes translate to having AI policy focus on establishing standards and creating parameters and scope for not just policy makers but users of AI and how it should be used in the government setting. Overall, these themes show a preliminary structure and solid foundation with considering the rapid development of AI within the technology sector, and will allow for further development that will focus on a robust foundation. 
 
## Future Considerations
Some considerations that should develop is how this documentation will relate to current legislation and how it can be applied, considering only 30% have connecting legislation this will need to develop quickly to keep up with the AI impact on the future of our worlds globalisation and trans-national relations and the legislation that follows. Additionally a standardised training for AI is lacking at 30%. General structure for various level of governments on AI training is important for a quickly developing technology to ensure security and accountiblity.
